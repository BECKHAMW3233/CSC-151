## Decisions and direction I made

These are the choices I made in putting this starting point together — the AI could
not have made any of these on its own, and each one shaped what got built:

1. **Chose a team to prototype with**: Carolina Panthers, based on proximity to
   Fayetteville, NC, since I had no strong team preference of my own and the group
   hadn't weighed in yet. Not a final decision — the app supports switching teams
   specifically so the group isn't locked into this choice.
2. **Set technical requirements up front**: Java only, GUI-based (not
   console/text), CSV-backed, and modular — matching the assignment's explicit
   asks, before any code existed.
3. **Rejected placeholder/sample data** once an initial version was working, and
   directed a full replacement with real, sourced 2026 roster/coaching/front-office
   data instead.
4. **Requested a zoom feature** for readability on a larger monitor, which led to
   the View menu and Ctrl+=/Ctrl+-/Ctrl+0 shortcuts.
5. **Asked directly "does this meet the requirements"** rather than assuming it
   did — which is what surfaced a real bug (changes weren't actually auto-saving,
   despite documentation claiming they were) before it could become a problem.
6. **Pushed on architecture correctness**: asked whether team names were
   hardcoded anywhere, which led to making the whole app data-driven with zero
   team-specific code — verified by an actual swap-and-revert test, not just an
   assertion.
7. **Scoped and directed a 32-team expansion**: decided to test whether the app
   could support every NFL team (not just one), specified the folder-per-team
   structure, and specified that data should be organized by division/region. This
   is exactly the kind of scope decision the group may want to revisit — it could
   just as easily be narrowed back to one team if that's what the group prefers.
8. **Set the data-quality bar** for that expansion: chose "full real rosters for
   every team" over lighter/placeholder alternatives, after being shown the
   tradeoff (a large research effort vs. a faster but thinner result).
9. **Kept the work moving through interruptions**: multiple background research
   agents hit usage limits mid-task; each time, I confirmed I wanted the work
   resumed rather than abandoned or restarted from scratch.
10. **Requested a real correctness audit** of the CSV data ("are the csv files
    proper and correct") instead of accepting "it works" at face value — this
    surfaced a real bug (a coach's age exceeded the edit dialog's allowed range)
    before it could crash the app for a real user.
11. **Requested this documentation** (comprehensive README, this contributions
    file) so the project's history and my role in it are traceable rather than
    assumed — and so the group has an honest record of what's actually done vs.
    still open, instead of a black box.

## What exists right now (subject to the group's review)

A full history with dates and reasoning is in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md); the
short version:

- A modular Java/Swing app (`model` / `data` / `gui` packages) covering Players,
  Coaches, and Support Staff, with full Add/Edit/Remove, search, and filtering.
- CSV-backed persistence with a hand-written, quote-aware CSV parser.
- Support for all 32 NFL teams, each with its own folder of 4 CSV files, selectable
  from a startup picker grouped by Conference/Division, switchable at runtime.
- Real 2026 data for all 32 teams (rosters, coaching staffs, front offices),
  researched from official team sites and ESPN.
- A zoom feature, auto-save on every edit, and a completely data-driven design with
  no team names hardcoded anywhere in the source.
- Along the way: found and fixed a real data-integrity bug (numeric bounds too
  tight for real NFL owners' ages/tenures) and a real rendering bug (the team
  picker went blank under this environment's specific display/GPU setup) —
  diagnosed with actual evidence (screenshots, stderr checks, timing tests) rather
  than guessed at.
